For more than a year I built projects quietly.  
I had a portfolio, some live sites, and a few client projects — but almost nothing that showed the actual thinking behind the work.

### 01 // Problem I faced

Most of my engineering work stayed private.  
When people looked at my portfolio they only saw finished results. They didn’t see the trade-offs, the bugs that took hours to fix, or the decisions I had to make under constraints.

I also realised that staying invisible was making it harder to get opportunities. People can’t recommend or hire someone they never see thinking in public.

### 02 // What I learned

1. **Visibility creates accountability**  
   Knowing that I will write about what I’m building forces me to be clearer and more deliberate.

2. **Writing improves understanding**  
   Trying to explain a problem in plain language quickly shows where my thinking is still weak.

3. **Start extremely simple**  
   I don’t need a complex system. A clean Next.js site with Markdown files is enough to begin.

4. **Consistency beats perfection**  
   Shipping a simple v1 and improving it over time is better than waiting until everything looks perfect.
